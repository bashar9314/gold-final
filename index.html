§ 02Methodology

Most retail trading bots are a hunch in code.

Someone notices a pattern, writes it up, runs one backtest on the same data that gave them the idea, and ships the screenshot. It looks extraordinary, because it was fitted to the exact year it was tested on. The Gold Desk was built the other way around — and the four steps below are the entire reason this page exists.

Sweep

Hundreds of parameter combinations, not one lucky set

Every setting the system exposes was swept across its plausible range and tested in combination — hundreds of variants, not the one that happened to look good on a chart.

The point isn't to crown the single highest-scoring set. It's to see the shape of the results. Performance that sits on a broad plateau, where neighbouring settings behave similarly, is a sign of something real. Performance that spikes at one exact combination and collapses on either side is a curve fit wearing a nice suit.

Out of sample

Tuned on one half of the year, proven on the half it never saw

The year was cut in two. Parameters were chosen using the first half only. Then those parameters were frozen — no further changes, no second look — and run across the second half, which played no part in any tuning decision.

This is the test almost nothing survives, and it's the only one that answers the real question: does the edge exist outside the data it was found in?

Cost

Real historical spread in every test, not an idealized number

Gold's spread widens at the rollover, around news, and at the thin end of the session — exactly when a lot of signals fire. A backtest run at a flat, friendly spread quietly deletes that cost.

Every result on this page charges the trade the real historical spread for that moment. Strategies that only work in a frictionless market don't make it out of testing.

Window

Hour by hour, day by day — where the edge actually is

Results were broken down by hour of the day and by day of the week, rather than judged as one blended number. A system can look profitable overall while quietly losing money in specific sessions and being carried by others.

Windows with no demonstrable edge are excluded from trading rather than hoped over. Fewer trades, in the hours that earned their place.

§ 03Execution

Four steps, and none of them are yours.

From signal to closed trade, the sequence runs without you in it. This is the order it happens in, every time.

01

Signal on a confirmed close

Conditions are evaluated only on candles that have actually closed. Nothing acts on a wick that's still moving, so a signal can't appear and then vanish.

02

Sent over a secure webhook

The signal travels over an authenticated webhook to the bridge running against your MetaTrader 5 terminal. No copying, no forwarding, no missed alerts.

03

Risk-sized entry

Position size is calculated from your live account equity and the distance to the stop — 2% of equity at risk per trade. Not a fixed lot that means something different at every balance.

04

Exit handles itself

Stop and target are attached at entry. The trade closes at one or the other on its own, whether you're at your desk, asleep, or nowhere near a screen.

You don't place the trade. You don't size it. You don't close it. Your job is to run the terminal and decide whether you're comfortable with the risk — the rest is the system's.

§ 05Questions

The questions worth asking before you buy.

Is this guaranteed to make money?

No. Nothing in trading is, and anyone who tells you otherwise is either lying or about to be wrong in public.

What the system has is a tested methodology and a defined risk model. It can lose. It will have losing weeks and losing months — the 24.8% drawdown in the test is not a rounding error, it's a real stretch of time where the account went backwards and the correct move was to keep running. Treat capital you put behind it as capital you can afford to lose.

Has this traded real money?

Yes — recently, and that's the honest limit of the claim. Live operation has been confirmed on a real account. What doesn't exist yet is a long real-money track record, and I'm not going to dress up a short one as a long one.

Everything on this page that carries a number comes from historical simulation, clearly labelled as such. The live record is being built now, in public, and it's the reason the price is what it is today.

Do I need to watch it?

No. That's the entire point of it. Signals fire on confirmed closes, entries are sized automatically, and exits are attached to the trade at the moment it opens.

You need MetaTrader 5 running and connected — most people use a cheap VPS so it isn't tied to their own machine — and you need to check in on it the way you'd check any account. What you don't need to do is sit in front of a chart waiting to click.

Why should I trust the backtest?

You shouldn't — not blindly. A backtest is the easiest thing in this industry to fake, and most of the ones you've been shown were fitted to the exact data they were tested on.

That's precisely why this one was built the way it was: parameters chosen on one half of the year and frozen, then run on a half they'd never seen; real historical spread charged on every trade; hundreds of parameter combinations tested to check the result sits on a plateau rather than a spike. You get the full validation report with the system, and you're welcome to pull it apart before you run a live lot.

Why is payment crypto only?

Because card processors classify trading software as high-risk. Accounts in this category get frozen mid-business, sometimes with funds held for months, and I'd rather not build on top of that risk. Crypto settles directly, with no intermediary who can switch it off.

Being straight about the other side of it: it also means you're sending an irreversible payment with no chargeback rights. So do your diligence first — ask me anything, get on a call, run the system on a demo account for as long as you want. I'd rather lose a sale than have you send money you weren't sure about.

What do I need to run it?

A MetaTrader 5 account with a broker that offers gold at a competitive spread, and a machine or VPS that stays online. Setup is documented step by step, and I'll help you through it during early access.

Start on a demo account for as long as you want. There's no argument from me about that — it's what I'd do.
